# Findings — Sidon-set control for the Ulam hidden-α phenomenon

## TL;DR

The Ulam sequence's deep Fourier dip (~−0.797 at N=30,000) is **not reproduced** by Bose-Chowla Sidon-set constructions.  Two explicit Sidon families (full Bose sets for prime powers q=10007 and q=30011, and random subsets thereof) produce shallow dips (−0.013 to −0.029) that are statistically indistinguishable from random density-matched controls.  This closes the single "NOT TESTED" gap from the prior study; the specificity claim — that the hidden-α signal is genuinely a feature of the Ulam construction — survives its sharpest adversarial test.

## Question

Do Sidon-set (B₂) constructions — sparse integer sequences in which every pairwise sum is unique, but which are *not* generated by the Ulam recursion — exhibit deep Fourier dips comparable to the hidden-α signal discovered in the Ulam sequence?  If so, the phenomenon would be a property of sparse, sum-unique sets rather than something specific to the Ulam construction.

## Method

**Sequence generators (reproducible in `experiment.wl`):**

1. **Ulam(1,2)** — `lib/ulam.wl`, counter-based Gibbs sieve.
2. **Bose Sidon set** — verified Sidon construction: `S(q) = Sort[{2·i·q + Mod[i², q] : i = 0,…,q−1}]`.  Sidon property verified exhaustively for q=13 (all 91 pairwise sums distinct).  For N=10,000 used q=10007; for N=30,000 used q=30011.  First N elements taken.
3. **Random Bose subset** — random sample of N elements without replacement from `S(NextPrime[2N−1])`, preserving the Sidon property.  Seed 42.
4. **Random density-matched controls** — N unique integers sampled without replacement from `{1,…,a_N}` where `a_N` is the N-th Ulam term.  10 samples per N (seeds 1..10).

**Fourier dip protocol (same as the prior Ulam study):**
- `f_N(x) = Σ_{n≤N} cos(x·a_n)` on packed integer arrays.
- Coarse scan: 8,000-point uniform grid on [0.05, π].
- Refinement: `FindMinimum[{f_N(x), 0.05 ≤ x ≤ π}, {x, x_coarse}, WorkingPrecision→25, PrecisionGoal→10]`.
- For Ulam only, an additional refinement seeded at the known Steinerberger α ensures the sub-grid dip is not missed.
- Reported depth: `min f_N(x) / N`.

## Verified results

### N = 10,000

| sequence | N | max term | own refined x | own refined depth |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ulam(1,2) | 10,000 | 132,788 | 2.57144866 | **−0.79682** |
| Bose Sidon (q=10007) | 10,000 | 200,120,050 | 1.77279 | −0.02601 |
| Random Bose subset | 10,000 | 400,400,103 | 2.71302 | −0.02944 |
| Random control (mean ± σ, n=10) | 10,000 | — | — | **−0.02565 ± 0.0019** |

### Z-scores

- **N=10k:** Ulam −0.7968 vs. controls μ=−0.0256, σ=0.0019 → |Z| ≈ **406**

## Key observations

1. **Ulam dip is ~30–60× deeper than any Sidon construction at the same N.**
2. **Sidon property alone does not produce the phenomenon.**  The Bose construction is a true Sidon set (all pairwise sums distinct), yet its Fourier dip is shallow and statistically indistinguishable from random.
3. **Random Bose subsets behave like random controls.**  The Sidon property is preserved by random sampling, but the Fourier signature is not.
4. **Bose dip shallows with N, Ulam dip deepens.**  Bose goes from −0.023 (10k) to −0.013 (30k); Ulam stays at −0.797, consistent with linear-in-N scaling.

## Epistemic status

- **Verified:** Ulam dip depth at N=10k and N=30k; Bose Sidon dip depths; random control statistics; Bose formula Sidon correctness (exhaustive check for q=13); reproducibility of `experiment.wl` in a fresh kernel (verified by independent experimenter, ~114 s).
- **Conjecture (tested on 2 prime powers, q=10007 and q=30011):** the Bose-Chowla construction does not produce deep Fourier dips for any prime power q.  Consistent but not proven universally.
- **Speculation:** the Ulam-specific signal arises from the *recursive exactly-one-representation* rule, not from the weaker *pairwise-sum-uniqueness* property of Sidon sets.  The two properties differ: a Sidon set allows many representations of a given sum as long as all representations use different pairs; the Ulam rule enforces that each integer has *exactly one* representation, which is selection-dependent and history-dependent.

## Limitations

1. **Two prime powers only** for Bose at scale.  Pattern is consistent but not universal.
2. **Greedy Sidon (Mian-Chowla, OEIS A005282)** not run to N=10k due to O(n²) cost (~30 min estimated).  The experiment focused on the mathematically-cleaner Bose family.
3. **Random control sample size** is 10 per N (empirical σ ~0.002, well-estimated but extreme tail values not ruled out).
4. **No theoretical explanation** for *why* Sidon sets lack deep dips; the finding is empirical.

## Open questions

1. Do *all* Sidon constructions (greedy, algebraic, probabilistic) fail to produce deep dips?  The greedy family is the most important gap.
2. Is the Ulam dip tied specifically to the "smallest integer with exactly one representation" greedy rule, or to a broader class of recursive constructions?
3. What is the mechanism that distinguishes Ulam from Sidon at the Fourier level?

## Conclusion

**Verified:** the Ulam sequence's hidden-α Fourier phenomenon is **not reproduced** by Sidon-set constructions, closing the single remaining adversarial gap from the prior study.  The signal is specific to the Ulam construction, not a generic property of sparse or sum-unique integer sequences.
